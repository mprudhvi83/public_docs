# Privacy Policy for Abacus Challenges

_Last updated: 23-Nov-2026

Abacus Challenges ("the app") is a mental-math practice app. This policy explains what happens to your information.

**What the app stores.** Profiles you create (name, avatar or a photo you choose from your device's photo picker), your
practice settings, and your test history and mistakes. All of it is stored **only on your device**. The app does not ask
for access to your location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage.

**What the app does not do.** The app has no accounts, no ads and no advertising tracking, and it does not use the
advertising identifier. Your profiles, practice history, mistakes, settings and photos never leave your device unless you
export or share them yourself. We (the developer) do not receive, sell or share them. The only two things the app can
send to us are the optional crash reports and usage statistics described below, and only if you turn them on.

**Crash reports (off by default).** Nothing is sent unless a parent or guardian turns this on. If it is on and the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Google's Firebase Crashlytics service so the developer
can fix the problem. A report contains technical information only: the error and stack trace, device model, Android
version, app version, the memory and storage state of the device, and a random installation identifier generated by
Firebase. It does not contain your name, profile names, practice history or photos, and it is not used for advertising.
This is **off by default**. You can turn it on, and off again at any time, in Settings > Privacy > "Send crash reports";
nothing is sent while it is off. For a child, a parent or guardian should make this choice.

**Usage statistics (off by default).** Nothing is sent unless a parent or guardian turns this on. If it is on, the app
sends usage statistics to Google's Firebase Analytics service, to learn which parts of the app are used: which screen was opened (for example "History") and which button was tapped (for example "Start
practice"). When a test starts, is paused or ends, it also sends how the test was set up (for example which operations,
how many digits, how many problems, the time limits) and a summary of how it went (how many problems were answered,
correct, skipped or timed out, the time spent, and whether it ended by finishing, running out of time or being ended
early), as numbers only. Each event also carries the technical data Firebase adds (device model, Android version, app
version, approximate region and a random installation identifier). It does not contain your name, profile names, the
actual problems or answers, your history or photos. This is **off by default**. You can turn it on, and off again at any
time, in Settings > Privacy > "Share usage statistics"; nothing is sent while it is off. It is separate from crash
reports. For a child, a parent or guardian should make this choice.

**Asking you to turn it on.** After the app has been opened on a few different days, it may show a message asking a parent or
guardian whether to turn on usage statistics; turning it on asks a short arithmetic question meant for a grown-up. You can choose "Not now"; it may ask again a few days later, up to five times in
total, and then never again. It never asks if you have already used the setting. This message is only shown on the home screen,
never during a test. The app keeps a small counter on your device (days of use, how many times it asked) to do this; it is not sent anywhere.

**Identifiers and IP address.** If you turn either option on, the random installation identifier used by crash reports and usage statistics is not your
name, your Google account or your advertising ID, and the developer cannot tell who you are from it. It is reset when you
uninstall the app. Like any internet connection, sending a report or statistics makes your IP address visible to Google's
servers, which Google uses to work out an approximate region. How Google handles it is described in the Google policies
linked below. We do not receive your IP address.

**Who processes this data.** Crash reports and usage statistics are processed by Google (Firebase) on our behalf, under
Google's terms and privacy policy (https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy and https://policies.google.com/privacy).
Data may be processed in countries other than yours. We use it only to fix crashes and improve the app, and we do not
sell it or use it for advertising.

**How long it is kept.** Crash reports are kept for about 90 days. Event-level usage statistics are kept for about 2
months. Because both are anonymous, we cannot look up or delete the data of one person; turning the switch off stops new
data being sent, and data already sent (only if you had turned it on) expires on the schedule above.

The app needs the internet permission (and the permission to check the network state) only for crash reports and usage
statistics, which are off unless you turn them on. It works fully offline.

**Export, import and sharing.** You can save your data to a file or share it with another app. A full backup may contain
profile names, history and photos. A single shared "tryout" contains its problems and, if you choose "include my answers
and stats", your results; its suggested name can include a profile name, which you can edit before sharing. Those files
go only where you send them, and you are responsible for who you share them with.

**Android backup.** If Android's backup is turned on for your device, Android may copy the app's data (profiles,
settings, history, photos) to your Google account, and may copy it to a new device when you set one up, according to your
device and Google account settings. This is done by Android and Google, not by us. You can turn it off in your device's
system settings.

**Children.** The app is made for people aged 6 and over, including children. It contains no ads, links, chat or other
social features, and has no accounts, so a child never has to give a name, email address or any contact detail. Profile
names, history and photos stay on the device, and a parent or guardian can see and delete them in the app. By default nothing
leaves the device. Only if a parent or guardian switches on crash reports or usage statistics in Settings > Privacy is
technical crash and usage data sent, as described above, tied to a random installation identifier and not to a name. We
do not use it to build a profile of a child, to show ads, or for anything except fixing crashes and improving the app. If you are a parent and
have questions, or want to ask us about your child's information, contact us at the address below.

**Your choices and deleting your data.**
- Crash reports and usage statistics are off unless you turn them on in Settings > Privacy; you can turn them off again at any time.
- Use Settings > "Clear history" to delete a profile's tests and mistakes, or delete a profile (and all its history) in
  the app.
- Uninstalling the app removes the data stored on the device. Data already copied to your Google account by Android
  backup, or saved in files you exported, is managed through your Google account or by deleting those files.
- If you have questions or requests about your information, contact us at the address below.
